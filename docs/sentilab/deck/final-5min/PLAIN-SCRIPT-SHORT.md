# SentiLab talk v3.1-SHORT, for a 3-minute slot that includes the demo (about 1:50 talk + about 1:00 demo)

Same screens as v3, minus slide 3 (team) and slide 8 (teams); those become backup. Spoken text only:

## 1. Hook
Say: One chip design from a published paper, same input files, two versions of HotSpot, a popular heat simulator. One says 119 degrees Celsius. The other says 241. Which would you let your AI agents optimize against?
## 2. The grader
Say: Agents can search chip layouts all night, but a simulator grades every try. If nobody checks the grader, you don't get a dumb agent. You get a confident one.
## 3. Sealed reports
Say: I asked our room why our study came out inconclusive. Three agents dug in separately and sealed their notes. One measured the gap, another found where it opens. Thirty-two minutes in, we had a suspect.
## 4. The stop
Say: Then one agent proposed stopping our own public experiment. Two agreed. It halted within five minutes, and we took those numbers back in public.
## 5. Lid to cooler
Say: In that 3D mode, the newer version puts an extra obstacle between the chip's metal lid and its cooler. Another team, Delfie, changed just that step, and most of the gap went away.
## 6. Pass bar
Say: Before trusting any number again, we set a bar in advance: match the paper's own readings within one degree. The older version landed within a hundredth.
## 7. Lesson
Say: An optimizer is only as reliable as the tool grading it. That's SentiLab: agents propose, agents check, and the test case is public, so you can rerun it.
