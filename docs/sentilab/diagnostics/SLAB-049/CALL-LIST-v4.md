## Operator authentication: the signed ledger and the tags

Two operator-owned facts make a call authentic. The code is `sundai_hotspot/gate0_anchor.py`.

- **The signed ledger.** Under the operator's root (`SLAB049_LEDGER_ROOT`), at
  `slab049-<this manifest's SHA-256>/`, a place derived from the manifest id and never from a record. Entries
  `000000.json`, `000001.json` and so on are canonical JSON, each signed with the operator's Ed25519 key and naming
  the SHA-256 of the entry before it. Entry 0 is the operator's grant: the grant id, this manifest, the call ids,
  `not_before_utc` and `not_after_utc`, a CPU cap of at most 600 CPU-s, and this very ledger directory, so a copy
  elsewhere, a replayed grant or a grant for another manifest is refused. Every other entry is one call's
  reservation or settlement; a call is reserved once and settled once, after its reservation. Every receipt is
  signed too.
- **The anchor: create-only tags** in this repository, under `refs/tags/gate0-<grant-id>/`, which a repository
  ruleset makes create-only (no update, no delete, no force-push, no bypass). The operator creates
  `reserve-<call-id>` before a launch and `settle-<call-id>` after settlement. validate-reference creates
  `launch-<call-id>` as its atomic claim. Each tag's commit message is JSON binding the grant, this manifest and the
  call; `launch` adds the signed reservation's hash, and `settle` the terminal status and the receipt and output
  hashes (a failure is settled too). Tags are read one at a time by exact name (`GET /git/ref/<ref>`, then its
  commit), never listed or matched by prefix.
- **Run-once holds without the ledger.** A launch tag cannot be created twice, so deleting, truncating or copying
  a ledger, or opening a fresh one, never readmits a launched call.
- **Key custody:** the private key and the tag-creation credential (`SLAB049_ANCHOR_TOKEN`) belong to warden's
  supervisor and never live in the repository. The code verifies against the pinned public key. Tests generate a
  throwaway key at run time and use an in-memory GitHub API.

## Enforcement: what the code enforces, and what the supervisor must

**In code, at the one entry (`validate-reference`), and in `record` and `report`** (`enforcement.in_code_at_entry`):

1. **Manifest identity:** it reads only `CALL-LIST-v4.json` and refuses it unless it is byte-identical to what
   this tree generates: the executed pins, every reference input and raw layout hash, the predicate, the limits,
   the accounting, the operator key and the anchor. It refuses the prior snapped manifest.
2. **Listed calls only:** the call id with its listed `--timeout`, and the engine, reference and grid when given;
   before the manifest's window closes; on the listed architecture. POSIX only, because the CPU limit is
   RLIMIT_CPU (Linux and macOS). Windows has no RLIMIT_CPU, and its equivalent, a Job Object process-time limit,
   is not implemented, so the entry refuses there. Never under CI allocation (`CI_ALLOCATION=1`, main's SLAB-046
   controller): there, every native HotSpot launch goes through `sundai_hotspot.ci_gate`, and SLAB-049 never
   solves in CI.
3. **The operator's authority, before anything is written:** the operator key must be the pinned public key's;
   the launch credential must be present; the signed ledger at the place derived from this manifest's id must
   verify, and its grant must name this manifest, this call, an open window, a cap of at most 600 CPU-s and this
   very ledger directory; the operator's reserve tag must exist and bind the call; no launch or settle tag and no
   ledger entry may exist for it; and the anchored aggregate (every call with a launch tag or a ledger entry,
   charged its full envelope or its measured CPU if larger) plus this call's envelope must fit the grant's cap.
   Any tag that cannot be read refuses: no launch on uncertainty.
4. **Evaluator identity:** the identity markers and SHA-256 are read from a private content-addressed copy (0500
   in a 0700 directory), and that copy is the file executed. The build provenance must describe exactly those
   bytes. A binary built with the CI entry guard (`CI_ALLOCATION=1`) is refused: it runs only through `ci_gate`.
   The shared libraries its own loader resolves must equal the build's, and are listed again after the run; a
   change settles the call as `runtime_changed`.
5. **Raw geometry:** the pinned raw-section hash (vendored copy and SLAB-048's record) and the continuous rules
   only; never the search lattice, never the annotation sections.
6. **The signed reservation before staging,** appended under the ledger's lock, which refuses a second
   reservation for the call. From then on the call is consumed and charged its full envelope, never reclaimed.
7. **The claim, immediately before launch:** validate-reference creates `gate0-<grant>/launch-<call>`
   (create-only). Only a successful create launches. An existing ref refuses. An uncertain create (no answer after
   sending) consumes the call with no launch, for the operator to reconcile. Nothing is retried from the absence
   of a settle tag. The order of one call is: reservation, staging, claim, launch.
8. **Per-process CPU and wall limits on every child,** soft = hard: the solver in its own session and process
   group, at RLIMIT_CPU and a wall deadline both equal to its timeout, the group killed while the solver is
   unreaped, then the solver reaped; the TAP generator at 3 CPU-s and 30 s; each loader listing at 1 CPU-s and
   10 s, in its own session, killed as a group at its deadline.
9. **Settlement:** on every exit path after the reservation, the signed receipt and the signed settlement entry,
   with the status, the measured CPU (children and self) and any overshoot. When no receipt can be written, the
   ledger still settles the call (receipt digest null). The settle tag's exact message is printed for the
   operator.
10. **Verification (`record` and `report`):** by admitted call id from the signed ledger, never caller-chosen
    directories; every tag read by its exact name. A missing settle tag gives INCOMPLETE; a tag that cannot be
    read gives ERROR; a ledger, receipt or tag that does not verify or disagrees gives FAIL. A completed call is
    re-derived from its raw files with the strict evaluator-bound reader: grid size, layer count, header format
    and complete block evidence. Nothing is taken from a stored solve record.

**Operator supervisor (warden), not in code** (`enforcement.operator_supervisor_contract`):

1. **Key custody:** generate and hold the Ed25519 private key; it never enters the repository. Pin its public key
   here (`slab049.OPERATOR_PUBLIC_KEY`) with a reviewed commit before the grant.
2. **The grant:** write the signed grant as ledger entry 0 (`gate0_anchor.open_ledger`: grant id, call ids,
   window, a cap of at most 600 CPU-s) under the operator's ledger root, one ledger per manifest, on storage the
   operator protects (append-only, backed up). Deleting or rolling it back cannot readmit a launched call (the
   tags refuse), but it loses the evidence.
3. **The anchor:** a repository ruleset on `refs/tags/gate0-*` that makes the namespace create-only (no update, no
   delete, no force-push, no bypass); `reserve-<call>` created before each launch; `settle-<call>` created after
   settlement with the printed message, failures included; a `claim_uncertain` call reconciled by settling it as a
   failure, never relaunched.
4. **Credentials:** `SLAB049_ANCHOR_TOKEN` (create tags in this repository) only in the supervisor's environment,
   never in the repository; `GH_TOKEN` with read access for `record` and `report`.
5. **A cumulative CPU budget** over the whole process group, descendants and validate-reference itself included,
   enforced while calls run (for example a cgroup v2 budget that kills at the limit). In code, RLIMIT_CPU is per
   process, and the parent's own CPU is measured and charged, not capped.
6. **Kill and reap if validate-reference itself dies** (SIGKILL, OOM, host loss): the call stays consumed (its

7. **A protected runtime image:** the loader, the libraries, the Python interpreter and its bytecode caches from
   immutable copies. In code, the executable is a private copy, but the libraries are listed before and after the
   run, which cannot detect a swap-and-restore during it.
8. **A trusted clock** for the grant window; the code reads the host clock.
9. **The final grant reconciles** the 600 CPU-s manifest cap with the room's 1,800 CPU-s envelope. Neither is
   raised here.

## Per-solve record

Every receipt holds the `solve_record` (`slab049.summarize_evaluation`). Its separately named fields are:

- `active_layer_grid_peak`: layer, peak and every argmax cell, each checked to lie inside the grid;
- `chiplet_centre_mapping`: each chiplet's GRID_CENTER value with its 1, 2 or 4 cells and weights, and the
  maximum;
- `historical_tap_stdout_selector`: TAP's `max(float(stdout.split()[3::2]))`, literal on HotSpot 6.0 stdout, and
  the same metric on the steady file;
- `spreader_sink_hottest_cell_rises`: the spreader and sink rise above ambient at their hottest cells, with the
  v7-signature pattern;
- `spreader_sink_drop_diagnostic`: the same-index spreader and sink values where their difference is largest
  (copper predicts about 1-2 K for this package; reported, not asserted);
- `same_index_stack_at_active_argmax`: at each active-layer argmax, the same grid index in layers 4 (chip),
  5 (TIM), 6 (spreader) and 7 (sink), with rises and layer-to-layer drops (reported, not asserted);
- `config_sha256`, `effective_package` and every input and output hash.

The grid file is parsed strictly (astra #56785), and its format is bound to the evaluator: the HotSpot 6.0
evaluator build and HotSpot 7 write every layer under headers. A truncated or headered layer-0 file fails.

## Pre-declared interpretation (frozen before any run)

**Validity first.** If any of these fails, stop and report; do not interpret.

- **Identity:** each binary carries its identity markers and matches its build provenance and runtime libraries.
  Each receipt names the binary that ran.
- **Inputs:** the staged inputs match `SHA256SUMS`, and the config is fresh and hashed.
- **Replays:** each replay's outputs are byte-identical to its primary's. For the native6 target this is part of
  the gate; a difference means the identity is not deterministic, and the gate fails.

**Gate zero (calls 1, 3, 9 and 10).**

- **PASS:** HotSpot 6.0 becomes the MATCH evaluator for grid 64. Its record unlocks
  `make_oracle("hotspot6", "search" | "headroom")` at grid 64.
- **FAIL, ERROR or INCOMPLETE:** MATCH stays blocked. A miss on the raw references is a miss of the paper's own
  layout. It is not attributed to snapping, and no snapped run can stand in for it.
- **Never** tune h, the sizes, the stack, the grid or the layouts toward 75.48 C, 115.94 C or any published number.

**The native7 comparator (calls 5, 7, 11 and 12).** Recorded as numerical evidence of the settled cause, with its
spreader and sink rises. It is simulated (HotSpot 7 detailed-3D coupling; withdrawn pending the HS6 correction)
and is not required to reach the native6 targets. What happens to numbers already produced on HotSpot 7 is an
owner decision, not part of this grant.

**Grid 32 against grid 64.** `|J32 - J64|` and the block-centre change are recorded per engine and reference as mesh
evidence. They do not gate.

**Known in-model differences** (context for reading a result, never a reason to adjust one):

- HotSpot's lumping in the package layers (ANALYSIS.md section 3);
- the raw decode's uncertainty, at most 0.0022 mm per centre, and the figure's fidelity to the authors' layout,
  which the figure cannot measure;
- TAP's die sizes and its power split are TAP's modelling assumptions; the shipped Ascend 910's two unpowered dummy
  dies are not modelled;
- the paper's statistic is the block-centre metric; our search objective J is the grid peak, a different
  statistic.
