- **Primary is raw.** The paper-match references are SLAB-048's raw decoded geometry (PR #57 at `063f659`): the

  and are still refused by the search path. Tests cover both sides.

- **Deferred, not omitted.** 75.47 C and 94.89 C are declared pending verification, with no calls. A required
  outcome that is missing, unsettled or incomplete gives INCOMPLETE, never PASS.
- **Outcomes by call id.** Every call id names its role (primary or replay), engine, reference and grid. Records
  and reports are assembled from the grant's ledger by call id, never from directories a caller chooses, so a
  replay can no longer overwrite its primary.
- **Explicit rows.** Every row carries `--call` and its own `--timeout`: 60 s at grid 64 and 15 s at grid 32. The

## Enforcement

**In code, at the one entry (`validate-reference`).**

1. **Manifest identity:** it reads only `CALL-LIST-v4.json` and refuses it unless it is byte-identical to what
   this tree generates: every source pin, reference input, raw layout hash, the predicate and the accounting. It
   refuses the prior snapped manifest.
2. **Listed calls only:** the call id with its listed `--timeout`, and the engine, reference and grid when given;
   before the window closes; on the listed architecture. POSIX only, because the CPU limit is RLIMIT_CPU (Linux
   and macOS). Windows has no RLIMIT_CPU, and its equivalent, a Job Object process-time limit, is not implemented,
   so the entry refuses there.
3. **Evaluator identity:** the identity markers and SHA-256 are read from a private content-addressed copy (0500
   in a 0700 directory), and that copy is the file executed. The build provenance must describe exactly those
   bytes. The shared libraries the loader resolves must equal the build's, and are hashed again after the run;
   a change settles the call as `runtime_changed`.
4. **Raw geometry:** the pinned raw layout hash and the continuous rules only; never the search lattice.
5. **Atomic admission:** under an exclusive ledger lock; one manifest per ledger; each call reserved once (O_EXCL,
   no retries). It refuses when the admitted count would pass 12, or when charged plus reserved CPU would pass
   600 CPU-s. Settled calls are charged their measured CPU, unsettled ones their reservation.
6. **Reserve before staging:** the reservation and the receipt are on disk (fsync) before inputs are generated or
   the solver starts.
7. **Per-process CPU and a separate wall deadline:** the solver runs in its own session and process group under
   RLIMIT_CPU equal to its timeout (soft = hard, so SIGKILL at the limit), with a wall deadline equal to its
   timeout. The TAP generator runs under RLIMIT_CPU 10 s and a 60 s deadline.
8. **Kill and reap:** after the solver exits or times out, its process group is killed while the leader is still
   unreaped (so the group id cannot be reused), then the solver is reaped.
9. **Settlement:** the receipt and the write-once ledger settlement are written durably on every exit path of this
   process. They keep the raw outputs, the status, the measured CPU (children and self) and any overshoot.
10. **Outcomes by admitted call id:** `record` and `report` read the ledger, never caller-chosen directories. A
    missing required outcome gives INCOMPLETE.

**Operator-supervisor contract (not in code).**

1. **Admission identity:** authenticate the operator and the host. Keep the ledger append-only and protected from
   deletion or rewrite (deleting a reservation would readmit its call). One ledger per grant.
2. **A cumulative CPU budget** over the whole process group, descendants and lifecycle work included, enforced
   while calls run (for example a cgroup v2 budget that kills at the limit). In code, RLIMIT_CPU is per process,
   and the ledger's cumulative check acts at admission only.
3. **Kill and reap if validate-reference itself dies** (SIGKILL, OOM, host loss): settle the call as unknown,
   charge its reservation, and never let it become a free replacement.
4. **A protected runtime image:** run the loader and libraries from immutable copies. In code, the executable is
   a private copy, but the libraries are hashed before and after the run, which cannot detect a swap-and-restore
   during it.
5. **A trusted clock** for the grant window; the code reads the host clock.
6. **The final grant reconciles** the 600 CPU-s manifest cap with the room's 1,800 CPU-s envelope. Neither is
   raised here.

## Per-solve record

Every receipt holds the `solve_record` (`slab049.summarize_evaluation`). Its separately named fields are:

- `active_layer_grid_peak`: layer, peak and every argmax cell, each checked to lie inside the grid;
- `chiplet_centre_mapping`: each chiplet's GRID_CENTER value with its 1, 2 or 4 cells and weights, and the
  maximum;
- `historical_tap_stdout_selector`: TAP's `max(float(stdout.split()[3::2]))`, literal on HotSpot 6.0 stdout, and
  the same metric on the steady file;
- `spreader_sink_hottest_cell_rises`: the spreader and sink rise above ambient at their hottest cells, with the
  v7-signature pattern;
- `spreader_sink_drop_diagnostic`: the same-index spreader and sink values where their difference is largest
  (copper predicts about 1-2 K for this package; reported, not asserted);
- `same_index_stack_at_active_argmax`: at each active-layer argmax, the same grid index in layers 4 (chip),
  5 (TIM), 6 (spreader) and 7 (sink), with rises and layer-to-layer drops (reported, not asserted);
- `config_sha256`, `effective_package` and every input and output hash.

The grid file is parsed strictly (astra #56785), and its format is bound to the evaluator: the HotSpot 6.0
evaluator build and HotSpot 7 write every layer under headers. A truncated or headered layer-0 file fails.

## Pre-declared interpretation (frozen before any run)

**Validity first.** If any of these fails, stop and report; do not interpret.

- **Identity:** each binary carries its identity markers and matches its build provenance and runtime libraries.
  Each receipt names the binary that ran.
- **Inputs:** the staged inputs match `SHA256SUMS`, and the config is fresh and hashed.
- **Replays:** each replay's outputs are byte-identical to its primary's. For the native6 target this is part of
  the gate; a difference means the identity is not deterministic, and the gate fails.

**Gate zero (calls 1, 3, 9 and 10).**

- **PASS:** HotSpot 6.0 becomes the MATCH evaluator for grid 64. Its record unlocks
  `make_oracle("hotspot6", "search" | "headroom")` at grid 64.
- **FAIL or INCOMPLETE:** MATCH stays blocked. A miss on the raw references is a miss of the paper's own layout.
  It is not attributed to snapping, and no snapped run can stand in for it.
- **Never** tune h, the sizes, the stack, the grid or the layouts toward 75.48 C, 115.94 C or any published number.

**The native7 comparator (calls 5, 7, 11 and 12).** Recorded as numerical evidence of the settled cause, with its
spreader and sink rises. It is simulated (HotSpot 7 detailed-3D coupling; withdrawn pending the HS6 correction)
and is not required to reach the native6 targets. What happens to numbers already produced on HotSpot 7 is an
owner decision, not part of this grant.

**Grid 32 against grid 64.** `|J32 - J64|` and the block-centre change are recorded per engine and reference as mesh
evidence. They do not gate.

**Known in-model differences** (context for reading a result, never a reason to adjust one):

- HotSpot's lumping in the package layers (ANALYSIS.md section 3);
- the raw decode's uncertainty, at most 0.0022 mm per centre, and the figure's fidelity to the authors' layout,
  which the figure cannot measure;
- TAP's die sizes and its power split are TAP's modelling assumptions; the shipped Ascend 910's two unpowered dummy
  dies are not modelled;
- the paper's statistic is the block-centre metric; our search objective J is the grid peak, a different
  statistic.
