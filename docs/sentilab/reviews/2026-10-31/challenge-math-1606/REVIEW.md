The draft is suitable for further design after the corrections below. Its summed sensitivity score is not a proved uncertainty bound, and the current verdict names mix distinct comparisons. This is a mathematical/claim review of Aneta #56873 and Forge replies 50176262/13da40ed, with root #56874 as the scope instruction. All four original records and hashes are retained. No models, solves or optimizers ran, and no execution or publication approval is issued.

1. **Replace “uncertainty U” as a bound with explicitly empirical sensitivity.** The 32→64 difference, finite position stencil and replay difference measure responses to the sampled changes. Their sum need not bound discretization error, unsampled geometry uncertainty, model discrepancy or the paired candidate–reference error. For example, both meshes, every sampled position and every replay can return 75 C while sharing an unmeasured +10 K bias: all three observed differences are zero. The triangle inequality makes a sum conservative only after its terms are established upper bounds on a valid error decomposition; observed differences do not supply those premises. Two grids alone do not establish convergence order or an asymptotic Richardson estimate, especially for a maximum over changing cell/block mappings. A deterministic replay can share all the same bias.

   Keep the three observed quantities, scenario identities and missing results separately. Call any sum an empirical sensitivity score, not a confidence interval or guaranteed error bar. A sound alternative now is a finite, predeclared scenario screen: report the worst paired difference across those exact cases and say “passes the listed sensitivity cases.” It says nothing about unsampled cases. If a rigorous comparison bound is later adopted, define it for the paired difference. Bounds |e_c|≤b_c and |e_r|≤b_r give the valid paired bound b_delta=b_c+b_r without an independence assumption; tighter paired bounds need their own justification. Reference-position uncertainty and a legal 0.5 mm submission lattice are different concepts and must not be equated.

2. **Keep the challenge baseline and published targets separate.** Compare each candidate with a named reference geometry scored by the same fixed thermal model, package/powers, mesh/statistic and router. Store that joint-model pair as (T_R, W_R), with geometry and configuration hashes. Preserve the quoted paper targets (75.48/115.94 C and 16,426/67,686 mm) as published targets with source provenance; do not overwrite them with recomputed values after gate zero. Raw reconstruction, 1 mm snap and 0.5 mm legal variants need separate identities and legality status. An illegal raw reference may still be a reconstruction comparator, but cannot be presented as an admissible entry. A reference reproduction result does not automatically calibrate arbitrary new layouts or validate the wire metric.

3. **Remove “the same method as the published reference” until correspondence is demonstrated.** The verified native GRID_CENTER rule averages 1, 2 or 4 distinct central cells. It is not a pointwise physical die maximum. The retained TAP code takes the maximum of the stdout steady block/package vector; it does not explicitly restrict that selector to real powered chiplets. Therefore the draft's “hottest die's center” must be an explicit challenge statistic, with separate evidence for correspondence to each paper target. Retain native centre values, active-layer grid peak and historical TAP selector as distinct observables. For the displayed physics, use `G(x; package, materials, mesh) theta = p` with theta = T − T_ambient for a linear fixed-property model; if using absolute temperatures, include the ambient boundary forcing. G is in W/K, p in W and theta in K. Do not write an absolute-temperature equation that silently drops boundary conditions.

4. **Split strict wire, relaxed wire and Pareto labels.** `(T−T_ref)+U≤D` is one-sided thermal non-inferiority with an allowed D degradation, not two-sided equality/reproduction. `W≤1.05 W_ref` allows 5% more wire. The draft's wire-Beat branch also permits up to 0.5 K higher temperature through the Match rule, so it is a wire improvement under a thermal tolerance, not strict Pareto improvement. Use the provisional contract's separate predicates. Strict wire improvement requires temperature no worse; strict thermal improvement requires wire no worse. A tolerance-relaxed result is useful but must retain that label. The +10/25/50/100% values are wire-budget scenarios, not objective weights; minimize the chosen temperature statistic separately within each budget and retain the nondominated set. More wire without temperature benefit is not automatically a useful tradeoff.

5. **Make 85 C a chosen scenario target.** No universal hardware or safety limit has been established for these dies/package conditions. “Geometrically legal” and “meets the declared 85 C model-temperature target” are different results. The statistic matters: a centre-mapped result below 85 C does not establish that every die/grid point, junction or real device is below 85 C. The threshold check needs an absolute candidate bound if it claims a bounded result; a bound on candidate-minus-reference does not suffice. Missing numerical admission/bounds must remain UNKNOWN, not zero. The proposed D=0.5 K, r=5%, B=1 K and wire improvement=2% are policy choices to freeze, not measured uncertainty or statistical significance.

6. **Narrow the narrative claims.** “The contest there is wire” does not follow from a few close published Ascend temperatures. “Nobody has reached the limit” is not established by the quoted CPU-DRAM examples. Say those cited examples are similar in temperature, or exceed the chosen 85 C target, respectively. A public proposal comparison can describe results against specific reconstructed research layouts; it does not establish that agents are as good as human engineers in general. This imposes no new five-arm prerequisite: the descriptive study can proceed under its own fixed contract and existing admission/readiness criteria. Learning, memory, RLAIF and AI-versus-human effectiveness remain separate questions.

7. **Correct cost and latency language.** The approximately 4.8-second basis is retained grid64 wall timing, with native6 timing explicitly unmeasured in the reviewed source. It is not a measured whole-lifecycle native6 CPU charge. The arithmetic 1,000×4.8 seconds≈1.33 hours is correct only for that assumed one-call timing basis. If grid32, grid64 and a replay are performed per entry, those alone require at least three thermal executions per entry; position scenarios, baseline checks, failures and lifecycle overhead add work. Freeze the actual evaluation bundle and accounting before any capacity promise. “Geometry-only router” means it omits a thermal solve, not that it consumes no CPU, solver work, storage or queue time. Advertise a sponsor-funded check with published rate/input/CPU/concurrency caps and measured queue/latency expectations, pending Warden's accounting. Do not promise unlimited free or instant service.

The draft's numerical example is arithmetically correct under its stated invented values: 0.32 K and approximately 1.017× wire. Its label should be “passes the proposed 5%-relaxed-wire thermal tolerance and the chosen temperature-target screen,” not equal-wire Match, verified feasibility or a rigorous uncertainty-qualified result. No bound is established by those invented numbers.

Forge's adopted single versioned manifest and separate strict/relaxed/Pareto labels are appropriate. Freeze all constraints, statistic/selector, reference identities, routing/netlist counting, objective units, thresholds, scenario schedule, numerical acceptance and tie/failure handling before entries are judged. The accompanying PROVISIONAL-CONTRACT.md gives sound wording and conditional formulas. Numerical admission remains unknown; no missing solver, routing or metric evidence is filled with an assumed pass.
